Concepts

Disguises

What a Disguise is, and what it is not.

A Disguise is a named, regulation-shaped bundle of masking defaults. It gives you a starting pipeline for a common compliance shape, such as HIPAA Safe Harbor identifiers or PCI cardholder data, instead of a blank YAML file.

What a Disguise is

Each Disguise is a template: a set of column-pattern-to-strategy defaults tuned for a specific regulation or use case. decoy templates show hipaa dumps a ready-to-edit pipeline that maps the identifier categories a HIPAA Safe Harbor review commonly cares about (names, dates, geographic subdivisions, medical record numbers, and the rest) onto strategies like faker, date_shift, fpe, and redact. You pick the Disguise, inspect the generated YAML, and tune it to your actual columns before running it.

Bundled starting points ship today for a generic default, HIPAA, PCI DSS, and GDPR shapes, alongside a bare-bones minimal template. Browse what's available with decoy templates list and preview one with decoy templates show <name>.

What a Disguise is not

It is not automatic classification. A Disguise does not scan your data and decide which template applies, and it does not apply itself. You choose a Disguise the same way you choose any other starting template: by name, because you know your data is HIPAA-shaped or PCI-shaped. This is the same disguises-as-presets stance that shapes STORM (see STORM): the tool surfaces defaults and findings, and a person decides what to run.

It is not a compliance certification. A Disguise named hipaa encodes one engineering-team's reasonable reading of a common identifier set. Running it does not, by itself, make a dataset HIPAA-compliant, GDPR-compliant, or compliant with any other regime. Whether a masked dataset satisfies a regulation as applied to your actual data is a determination for you and your counsel, not a claim this tool makes.

It is not a fixed, uneditable bundle. Every Disguise resolves to plain pipeline YAML. Nothing stops you from deleting a column entry your data doesn't have, swapping a strategy, or adding columns the template didn't anticipate. Treat the output of decoy templates show as a draft, not a final answer.

Why bundles exist at all

Writing a masking pipeline column by column from scratch is slow, and it's easy to forget an identifier category a regulation actually cares about. A Disguise front-loads that knowledge into a template so the first draft of your pipeline already covers the common cases; you're editing toward correctness for your data, not starting from an empty file.

See decoy explain disguises for the command-line summary, and how to scan for PII for the recipe that pairs a STORM scan with picking a Disguise.
