# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Decoy serves developers, data engineers, platform teams, and security or compliance reviewers who need realistic non-production data without exposing raw personally identifiable information. The primary entry point is a CLI for developers working locally and in CI. The Platform extends the same engine for enterprise teams that need shared workflows, approvals, run history, access controls, and audit evidence.

## Product Purpose

Decoy turns production-shaped data into safe, realistic, join-preserving data for development, testing, CI, and analytics. It makes the transformation explicit and reviewable, runs inside the customer's infrastructure, and records evidence of what happened. The website should help a technical buyer understand the workflow, trust the product boundary, and choose between the CLI and Platform without obscuring that both use the same engine.

## Brand Personality

Professional, clean, and technically credible. The tone is precise and direct. It should feel like a dependable developer tool that can stand up to enterprise review, not an enterprise product wearing a developer costume.

## Anti-references

Avoid flashy AI-SaaS visuals: decorative glows, abstract gradients, speculative dashboards, glass panels, and motion without explanatory value. Avoid generic security imagery, stock-photo teams, and illustrations that make technical claims look metaphorical. Do not bury the CLI beneath enterprise messaging or imply capabilities that are not shipped.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the real workflow.** Prefer CLI output, pipeline configuration, product screens, tables, and evidence artifacts over abstract explanation.
2. **CLI first, Platform when teams need it.** Keep the developer path primary while showing how the Platform adds collaboration, governance, and repeatability.
3. **Make transformation visible.** Use concrete before-and-after data and relationship-preserving examples so visitors can see what changed and what stayed intact.
4. **Evidence over spectacle.** Visuals should prove a capability, clarify a boundary, or explain a decision. Decoration alone is not enough.
5. **Professional clarity.** Preserve a calm, precise hierarchy that works for both hands-on engineers and enterprise reviewers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, semantic structure, readable contrast, meaningful alternative text, and usable layouts under zoom and narrow viewports. Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and color must never be the only way raw, safe, warning, or success states are communicated.
