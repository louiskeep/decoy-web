---
title: What Decoy cannot prove
description: The honest boundary around what Decoy guarantees, so you don't rely on a promise it doesn't make.
---

Decoy is a practical de-identification and synthetic-data tool. It applies
recognized transformation primitives (masking, hashing, format-preserving
encryption, generalization, suppression, synthesis) and preserves structural
properties like foreign keys and determinism. This page is the honest
boundary: the things Decoy does not prove, so you don't rely on a guarantee
it doesn't make.

## It provides a formal privacy guarantee in exactly one place

Masked outputs carry no differential privacy, no epsilon, and no
mathematical bound on re-identification risk. STORM's re-identification-risk
score is a heuristic diagnostic to help you assess a dataset; it is not a
proof.

The one formal mechanism is `decoy fit --epsilon`: a distribution snapshot's
counts are released with per-count noise under differential privacy, exact
quantiles and means are removed, and min/max collapse to histogram-edge
resolution. Read that scope narrowly:

- The privacy budget is per column histogram. A snapshot of several columns
  composes sequentially, so releasing k columns spends roughly `(k + 1) *
  epsilon` of total budget.
- Bin edges and category labels stay data-dependent: the histogram range
  comes from the real min and max, and categorical top values can carry real
  category strings when explicitly allowed. A fully data-independent release
  is a known follow-up, not current behavior.
- Joint (multi-column) distributions are not covered by `--epsilon` in the
  current version; there is no composition accounting for releasing
  marginals and a joint table under one budget yet.
- Nothing downstream of the snapshot inherits the guarantee. Generation
  samples deterministically from the noisy artifact, and masking is entirely
  outside this mechanism.

If your use case requires a formal privacy guarantee over the masked or
generated data itself, Decoy alone does not supply it. Differential privacy
in Decoy is scoped to the `--epsilon` snapshot path described above; it is
not a property of masked output in general.

## It does not certify legal or regulatory compliance

Decoy ships configuration bundles named after regulations, such as a HIPAA
Safe Harbor Disguise that targets the 18 identifier categories a Safe Harbor
review commonly names. These are engineering aids that encode one reasonable
interpretation of an identifier set. They are not a compliance
certification, not legal advice, and not a determination that any given
output meets a regulation as applied to your data. Whether a masked dataset
satisfies HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA, or any other regime is a determination for you
and your counsel, considering your data, your context, and the
residual-risk analysis the regulation requires. Running a bundle named
`hipaa` does not by itself make a dataset HIPAA-compliant.

## It does not guarantee semantic correctness of free text

Free-text redaction finds and replaces PII spans using pattern-and-hint
detection, optionally backed by named-entity recognition. Detection is
best-effort: it can miss an identifier the detectors don't recognize (a
false negative), or replace a span that wasn't actually sensitive (a false
positive). Decoy does not understand the meaning of free text and does not
guarantee that every identifier in a notes column has been found, nor that
the surviving text stays semantically coherent after replacement. Treat
free-text output as reduced-risk, not proven-clean, and review it where the
stakes warrant.

## It does not validate that your configuration matches your intent

Decoy validates a config against its schema and runs what the config says.
It does not know which columns in your data are actually sensitive. If a
config leaves a sensitive column on `passthrough`, or masks the wrong
column, the run succeeds and the output leaks. Use STORM to find candidate
PII and use post-mask integrity checks to look for residual identifiers, but
the mapping from your data's actual sensitivities to a correct config is
yours to get right; see [STORM](/docs/concepts/storm) for how that review
step is meant to work.

## The STORM classifier does not de-identify anything

STORM's column classifier detects which semantic type a column is likely to
contain (SSN, email, a diagnosis code, and so on). It does not mask, redact,
transform, or remove any values. A column classified as `ssn` still contains
SSNs in the clear until a masking step in your pipeline explicitly
transforms it.

The classifier is an advisory signal, not a ground truth. It can have false
negatives (PII columns it misses) and false positives (non-PII columns it
flags). It was trained without any real PII entering its weights, but that
is a training-data hygiene property, not a differential-privacy guarantee
about what the model outputs.

## Format-preserving encryption has known edge cases

Format-preserving encryption (`fpe`) is designed to permute in-charset
characters and pass separators through unchanged. Two edge cases are worth
knowing:

- A value made entirely of characters outside the configured charset is
  routed through a fallback so it isn't emitted verbatim. A value with a
  **mix** of in-charset and out-of-charset characters is not covered by that
  fallback: the in-charset characters are permuted, but the out-of-charset
  characters pass through in the clear, by the same "separators pass
  through" rule that makes FPE useful for values like formatted phone
  numbers in the first place. If a column's real alphabet is wider than the
  charset you configured (for example, a mostly-numeric ID that sometimes
  carries letters), those letters can leak. Widen the charset to cover the
  column's full alphabet, or use a strict orphan-value policy that rejects
  values outside the expected shape instead of passing them through.
- A verbatim-leak check that looks for characters staying unchanged across
  rows only has signal at character positions with enough variety in the
  source data to distinguish "leaking" from "legitimately low-entropy and
  correctly preserved" (for example, a checksum digit that is genuinely
  always the same value). A leak confined entirely to a low-entropy position
  is not caught by that check; it surfaces as an explicit skip rather than a
  silent pass, but it is still on you to widen the charset for columns with
  a mixed or unusual alphabet.

## Correlation checks depend on the strategy

A value-changing mask (`fpe`, `hash`, `code_set`, and similar) relabels
every cell, so a naive comparison of masked and source distributions can
show near-zero similarity even when the underlying correlation structure was
preserved perfectly, because the labels no longer overlap. A relabel-aware
statistic (comparing contingency counts rather than value labels) is needed
to check correlation preservation across a bijective mask correctly, and
that is what Decoy's acceptance checks use for categorical column pairs.
That check does not extend to high-cardinality continuous columns, or to
strategies that merge distinct source values together (`bucketize`,
`geo_generalize`, and similar), since those change the contingency structure
itself rather than just relabeling it.

## What it does do

To be clear about the other side: Decoy does give you deterministic,
reproducible masking; foreign-key and join preservation across tables; a
catalog of standard de-identification transforms; PII detection and risk
profiling; and synthetic-data generation. Those features are real and
exercised by the project's test suite. This page exists so the strength of
those features is not mistaken for guarantees Decoy does not make.
