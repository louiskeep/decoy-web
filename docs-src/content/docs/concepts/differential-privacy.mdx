Decoy can give you a formal **differential privacy** guarantee in one specific
place: a distribution snapshot fitted with `decoy fit --epsilon`, and the
synthetic data generated from it. This page explains what that guarantee is,
where the budget lives, and what it does not cover.

It comes in two steps, and the privacy budget appears in both:

1. **Fit a DP snapshot.** `decoy fit --epsilon --delta`, with each column
   declared as a typed carrier, releases a `dps-marginal/v3` artifact instead
   of the exact snapshot. The counts in that artifact are noised under
   differential privacy.
2. **Generate against a budget ceiling.** In the pipeline, `global_settings.dp`
   declares that the generated output must be DP and sets an enforced
   `(epsilon, delta)` ceiling. At compile time Decoy verifies every statistical
   column was fitted with DP and that the total spend stays under the ceiling.

## The guarantee

The fit reads your source once and releases, under differential privacy:

- A noisy row count.
- For each numeric column, a noisy histogram over a domain you declare.
- For each categorical column, a noisy count per category, with rare
  categories dropped by a noisy threshold.

The guarantee is defined under the **add-or-remove-one-row** model: the
released counts are indistinguishable, up to the `(epsilon, delta)` budget,
between two datasets that differ by one row. OpenDP builds and certifies each
noisy measurement, and the `dp_accounting` library composes those certificates
into the fit's total spend. Decoy does not invent its own noise formula.

**This is a mathematical bound on the released statistics, not anonymization.**
Differential privacy bounds what the published counts can reveal about any one
row. It does not promise the output is scrubbed of all risk, and it is the
right tool only when you want a budgeted, measured limit on disclosure from a
column's distribution.

## When to use it

Use a DP fit when:

- You need to generate synthetic data and share or publish it outside the
  team that holds the source.
- You want a formal, parameterized privacy guarantee rather than
  security through obscurity.

Do not rely on Decoy's DP for:

- Masked output. Masking carries no DP guarantee and is outside this mechanism.
- Dates or free text. These cannot be DP carriers (see the carrier list below).
- Preserving the source distribution exactly. DP adds noise, which lowers
  fidelity.

## Typed carriers: what a DP fit can release

Every column in a DP fit is declared as one of three carriers, and only these
three are eligible:

- **`number`** (`--dp-number COL:LO:HI`): a numeric column, binned over the
  data-independent domain `[LO, HI]` **you** supply. Values outside the domain
  clamp to the bounds. The domain is not read from the data, because reading
  the real min and max would itself leak at zero budget.
- **`text`** (`--dp-text COL`): a categorical string column, released as noisy
  per-category counts. Categories whose noisy count falls under the threshold
  are dropped.
- **`flag`** (`--dp-flag COL`): a boolean column.

Every source column must be declared with a carrier, or the fit refuses. Pass
`--dp-allow-omit` to deliberately leave undeclared columns out of the release.
Dates (`--parse-dates`) and joint tables (`--joint`) are not compatible with
`--epsilon`.

## Epsilon and delta: the privacy budget

The guarantee is parameterized by two numbers:

**Epsilon (ε)** sets how much noise is added. Smaller epsilon means more noise
and stronger privacy; larger epsilon means less noise and higher fidelity.
Typical values run from about 0.1 to 10.

- ε = 0.1: very strong privacy, low fidelity.
- ε = 1: a common middle choice.
- ε = 10: weak privacy, high fidelity.

**Delta (δ)** is the small probability that the guarantee does not hold. Use a
small positive number below 1, typically 1e-6 or smaller. A common rule is to
keep delta below 1/n, where n is your row count.

### The budget is per release, not per column

The requested `(epsilon, delta)` is the budget for the **whole fit**, covering
every declared column together. Internally the fit schedules one noisy query
for the row count, one per numeric column, and two per categorical column, then
allocates and composes them so the total spend stays within the epsilon you
asked for. You do not pay a separate epsilon for each column, and the spend does
not grow by a per-column multiplier.

What composes is **distinct releases**. Each fit mints one release with its own
identity. When a pipeline's generated columns draw from several independent DP
fits, their budgets add up and are checked against the `global_settings.dp`
ceiling. Columns that share a single release are charged once. If the composed
spend exceeds the declared ceiling, the pipeline fails to compile with
`dp_budget_exceeded`.

## Configuration

A DP pipeline has two parts: the fit command that produces the snapshot, and
the pipeline YAML that declares the ceiling and samples from it.

Fit the snapshot with a carrier for each column:

```console
$ decoy fit patients.csv --output patients.snapshot.json \
    --epsilon 1.0 --delta 1e-6 \
    --dp-number age:0:120 \
    --dp-text diagnosis_code \
    --dp-flag readmitted
```

Then declare the ceiling in the pipeline and sample the snapshot with
`type: statistical` columns:

```yaml
version: 1

global_settings:
  seed: 42
  dp:
    epsilon: 1.0
    delta: 1e-6

sources: {}

tables:
  - name: patients
    row_count: 1000
    generate_columns:
      - name: patient_id
        type: sequence
        start: 10000
      - name: age
        type: statistical
        snapshot_file: ./patients.snapshot.json
        source_column: age
      - name: diagnosis_code
        type: statistical
        snapshot_file: ./patients.snapshot.json
        source_column: diagnosis_code
      - name: readmitted
        type: statistical
        snapshot_file: ./patients.snapshot.json
        source_column: readmitted

targets:
  patients:
    type: file
    format: csv
    path: ./patients_synthetic.csv
```

Key points:

- **`global_settings.dp` holds only `epsilon` and `delta`.** It is the
  enforced ceiling for the generated output. The per-column domains and
  carriers are declared at fit time, not here.
- **Each statistical column names its snapshot and source column.** The budget
  is set when you fit, not in the generate step.
- **`allow_real_categories: true` and `high_cardinality: true` are rejected**
  under `global_settings.dp`, because both would release real observed
  vocabulary instead of the certified label set and silently void the
  guarantee.

## What the guarantee covers

The formal guarantee applies to:

- The released per-column counts: numeric histograms and categorical counts.
- Synthetic rows sampled from the noisy snapshot.

It does **not** cover:

- Column names or the schema. These are treated as public metadata.
- Foreign-key structure. Also public structure.
- Masked output. Masking is entirely outside the DP boundary.
- Conditional or joint generation (`condition_on`), which is rejected under DP
  because joint distributions are not released privately in this build.
- Dates and free text, which are not eligible carriers.

## What the guarantee does not mean

**DP does not mean zero leakage.** It means bounded leakage. An attacker with
the synthetic data learns something, but only up to the epsilon budget.

**DP is not anonymization.** Privacy and utility trade off. More noise makes
the output safer to publish and less faithful to the source. You still review
the output and decide whether it is safe for your use.

**DP in Decoy measures by row, not by person.** The guarantee covers removing
one row. If one person has fifty rows (claims, lab results), removing that
person is a fifty-row change, far beyond the one-row guarantee. For
multi-record data, per-person privacy is weaker than the per-row epsilon
suggests. Decoy's unit is the row.

## Comparing to a non-DP fit

A plain `decoy fit` (no `--epsilon`) captures exact quantiles and counts with
no noise: no privacy guarantee, generally higher fidelity, and faster, because
there is no OpenDP measurement to run. A DP fit trades that fidelity and speed
for the formal bound.

A recurring misconception: DP is not what makes the output resemble the source.
Fitting does. DP is what makes the resemblance safe to publish.

## When fidelity drops

Turning on DP normally lowers fidelity against the same fit without it. The main
reasons:

- Noise is added to histogram counts and category counts.
- Rare categories are dropped when their noisy count falls under the threshold.
- Lower epsilon (more noise) pushes the output further from the source.

To improve fidelity under DP you can raise epsilon (weaker guarantee), raise
delta (higher failure probability), release fewer columns, or narrow a numeric
domain so each bin covers less range.

## Usage in practice

1. **Plan your budget.** Decide epsilon and delta from your sensitivity and use
   case. Consult your privacy or security team for a high-stakes release.
2. **Declare carriers at fit time.** Give every column a `--dp-number`,
   `--dp-text`, or `--dp-flag`, and a data-independent domain for each numeric
   column.
3. **Set the ceiling and generate.** Put the matching `(epsilon, delta)` in
   `global_settings.dp` and sample the snapshot with statistical columns.
4. **Review and test.** Check that the output is useful for your downstream
   task. DP adds noise, so utility may be lower than a non-DP fit.
5. **Publish with the budget in hand.** Once fidelity is acceptable, the
   synthetic data carries the `(epsilon, delta)` guarantee.
